Toilet Overflow Cleanup in Tempe: What to Know

An overflowing toilet seems like something towels can handle, except the water keeps moving past the tile. It runs under the baseboard, into the wall and through the gap around the drain pipe. What matters most is what was in it. Clean water is what comes out of the supply line or the tank. Anything that rose out of the bowl is a different matter and must be treated as contaminated.

The first thing our Tempe team does is identify the water category, since every later step depends on it. Clean water means extraction and drying. For gray or black water we wear protective gear, remove soaked porous items such as carpet pad, and disinfect each surface the water reached. Then we check the subfloor, the wall bases and the room underneath with moisture meters so nothing is left wet.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Stop the WaterFind the shutoff valve behind the toilet and turn it clockwise as far as it goes. A stuck valve can be worked around by taking off the tank lid and lifting the float, which stops the fill.
  2. Do Not Flush AgainDo not flush again or run sinks, showers or the washer. If the drain line is blocked, any water you send down can come back up.
  3. Stay Out of Contaminated WaterWhen there is waste in the water or it came out of the bowl, keep pets and children away, and put on waterproof shoes and gloves before stepping in it.
  4. Look at the Ceiling UnderneathFor an upstairs toilet, look at the ceiling underneath. A ceiling that sags or drips means turning off that room's power and moving your things clear.
  5. Block the SpreadUse old towels to keep clean water from reaching carpet or hallways. Contaminated water should be left to a crew that has the right disinfectants and equipment.
Coverage

Insurance and Toilet Overflow Cleanup

Many homeowners policies cover a toilet overflow when it is sudden and accidental, such as a failed fill valve or an unexpected clog. Water that backs up from the sewer or a drain is often excluded unless you added a backup endorsement, and that can apply to an overflow caused by a blocked main line. If the toilet leaked at its base for months, that damage is usually not covered. Read your policy or call your agent to find out which situation applies to you.

Common Causes

What Causes It

  • Tree roots or a collapsed section in the sewer line that slows every drain in the house.
  • Too much paper, wipes or a child's toy flushed and lodged in the trap.
  • A clog in the trap or drain line that sends bowl water over the rim.
  • Fill valves or flappers that stick, so the tank never stops running.
  • Clean water flooding the bathroom from a supply line that failed or a tank that cracked.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Downstairs Ceiling Damage

In a second story bathroom, water travels down along the drain pipe and collects on top of the ceiling drywall below, which stains, sags and in some cases collapses.

Bacterial Contamination

Water from the drain or bowl deposits bacteria in drywall, baseboards, carpet and pad. Because disinfecting porous material is not reliable, it usually has to come out.

Vanity and Drywall Wicking

Drywall edges and the vanity base stand in the water and draw it up like a wick. The wall can be wet several inches above the floor while the paint still looks fine.

Odor That Returns

Contaminated water that dries inside a wall base or under flooring leaves a smell that returns in humid weather until the source material is taken out.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Identify the Category

First we determine if the source was the bowl and drain or the supply side. Proper containment and protection are put in place before cleanup starts.

2

Extract and Remove

Standing water is extracted first. Contaminated drywall, baseboards or carpet pad come out where necessary, and the toilet is lifted when water has reached beneath the flange and base.

3

Disinfect Surfaces

We clean the subfloor, fixtures, wall bases and floors, then treat them with antimicrobial products. When the water is Category 3, we finish this step before turning on any drying equipment.

4

Dry and Verify

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the bathroom and any affected room below. Moisture readings continue until the ceiling and subfloor return to normal levels.
