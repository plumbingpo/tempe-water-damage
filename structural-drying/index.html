Structural drying is the most important phase of water damage restoration, and the one untrained responders most often get wrong. After extraction removes the standing water, significant moisture remains trapped in structural materials, including drywall, wood framing, subflooring, concrete, insulation and other building components. This trapped moisture must be scientifically removed through controlled evaporation and dehumidification to prevent the mold growth, wood rot, material degradation and odor problems that develop when structures remain wet.

The structural drying technicians at Tempe Restoration are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. That knowledge lets them work out where equipment goes, what temperature to hold and how air should move so evaporation is as effective as possible on every job. Plaster, concrete, hardwood and drywall each dry at their own rate, so each needs its own approach to get down to an acceptable moisture level with no added damage.

Every day of the drying process, we check moisture using thermal imaging cameras, thermo-hygrometers and calibrated moisture meters. Readings are recorded from start to finish in a detailed drying log, which shows moisture dropping step by step in every affected material. Equipment is adjusted daily based on these readings to optimize drying efficiency. Drying is complete only when all affected materials reach acceptable moisture standards as defined by IICRC S500 guidelines, not when surfaces simply feel dry to the touch.

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's what you can expect when you choose Tempe Restoration:

How to Prevent Structural Drying Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • New odors or wet areas during the drying period should be reported to your technician right away
  • Do not place personal items on top of drying equipment or block the airflow from air movers
  • Moisture can sit deep in a material whose surface feels dry, so rely on the meter readings
  • Let air movers and dehumidifiers run continuously, 24/7, until the technician removes them
  • Plan for daily access so readings can be taken and equipment adjusted
  • Shut the doors and windows of any room under drying, which keeps dehumidification working well

What a Professional Brings to Structural Drying

There is real science behind structural drying, which is why guessing does not work. Secondary mold damage most often traces back to someone who put down fans and hoped things would dry. To do it professionally, you have to know how each material takes on and gives up moisture, work out the grain depression, meaning the moisture removal capacity, for the job, and check progress every day. Lacking that understanding, people either stop too soon and leave moisture for mold, or dry too hard and harm wood and other materials.

The equipment used for professional structural drying is fundamentally different from consumer products. A commercial LGR dehumidifier, short for low-grain refrigerant, pulls 10 to 20 times as much moisture out of the air as a residential one, while high-velocity air movers set up the exact airflow that speeds evaporation off structural surfaces. Machines like these cost a great deal and take training to use well, which explains why professional results are so much better than a do-it-yourself effort.

Signs It Is Time for Structural Drying

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Wood flooring with boards that cup, crown or pull apart
  • High humidity in affected areas despite efforts to ventilate
  • No visible water left, yet walls or floors still feel cold and damp
  • After water damage, a meter shows elevated moisture in the floor, wall or ceiling
  • Window glass that fogs up near the affected rooms
  • Wall finishes that start to peel or bubble in the days after water damage
  • Stained, swollen or soft spots in the drywall
