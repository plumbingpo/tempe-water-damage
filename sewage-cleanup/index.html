A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. Across Tempe, Tempe Restoration answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

Water damage comes in categories, and sewage sits in the worst one: Category 3, called black water, the highest level of contamination and danger. This classification requires the most stringent cleanup protocols, including full personal protective equipment, containment barriers, removal of all porous materials that contacted sewage and thorough disinfection of every remaining surface. Our technicians follow IICRC S500 standards for Category 3 water damage so your property is restored to safe occupancy standards.

The cleanup process involves removing all standing sewage, extracting contaminated water from structural materials, removing and properly disposing of every porous material that contacted sewage (carpet, padding, drywall, insulation), thoroughly disinfecting hard surfaces, applying antimicrobial treatments and completing structural drying to prevent secondary mold growth. To finish, thermal foggers and hydroxyl generators of industrial grade deodorize the space so the sewage smell does not linger.

Sewage Cleanup FAQ

A blocked sewer line on the property is the most common starting point. Typical culprits are tree roots entering the pipe, grease hardening inside it and wipes or similar items flushed down a toilet. Sometimes an aging pipe cracks or caves in, or a storm overwhelms the city sewer and forces sewage up the floor drains. A camera run through the line after cleanup identifies the actual cause.
Some things can be kept and others cannot. Glass, sealed tile, metal and other hard surfaces that do not absorb water are normally salvageable after disinfection. Anything porous the sewage touched must go, which covers mattresses, upholstered furniture, drywall, insulation, carpet and padding. For insurance purposes, our crew keeps a list of everything that had to be removed.
We typically recommend temporary relocation during sewage cleanup, especially if the contamination in your home is extensive. There are health risks from pathogens that become airborne and from the products we use to decontaminate. Our recommendation will depend on what we find in your particular home.
Emergency sewage removal and decontamination typically takes 1 to 3 days to complete. Complete restoration, including material replacement, may take 1 to 3 weeks depending on the extent of damage. Throughout, the focus is on returning your home to a safe, livable state without delay.
Typical pricing for this work in Tempe is $2,000 to $10,000 and up, with the size of the affected area and the degree of contamination setting the number. Small contained backups may cost $1,500 to $3,000, while extensive sewage flooding can exceed $15,000 including material replacement and reconstruction.

Why Hire a Professional for Sewage Cleanup?

No homeowner should try to clean up sewage personally. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. People exposed to the pathogens in sewage can become seriously ill with skin and respiratory infections, hepatitis or gastrointestinal disease.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Food waste, oils and grease block pipes, so put them in the trash and not the sink
  • Have your sewer line inspected with a camera every 2 to 3 years to catch potential problems
  • In an emergency a plumber needs the cleanout, so learn where yours is and keep it in good shape
  • Use drain strainers in showers and sinks to catch hair and debris
  • Wipes clog sewer lines even when the label says flushable, so only toilet paper goes down the toilet
  • Do not wait on a slow drain, which can turn into a full blockage

When to Call for Sewage Cleanup in Tempe

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Dark residue from sewage left on the basement floor or ringing a drain
  • Several fixtures backing up at once instead of only one
  • Gurgling sounds from toilets or drains when other fixtures are used
  • Toilet water with waste in it soaking into carpet or running across the floor
  • A cleanout that weeps sewage, or wet foul patches in the lawn along the sewer path
  • Unexplained illness or gastrointestinal symptoms among household members
  • Damp patches on a wall or floor that also smell foul
