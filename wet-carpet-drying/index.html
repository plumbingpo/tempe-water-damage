Wet Carpet Drying Help in Tempe

Carpet holds far more water than it looks like it does. The face fibers can feel only damp while the pad underneath is soaked like a sponge and the subfloor below it is wet too. Towels and a shop vacuum reach the top layer and little else. Our team in Tempe extracts the water, checks every layer with moisture meters, and sets up drying that reaches all the way down.

Saving a carpet comes down to how long it stayed wet and whether the water that soaked it was clean. When a clean supply line is the source and we arrive early, drying the carpet where it lies usually works. Gray water from an appliance often means the pad goes and the carpet gets cleaned and treated. Both layers, carpet and pad, are removed when the water was sewage or came in from outside flooding.

What It Looks Like

  • Dampness that spreads outward on its own, well after the leak was stopped
  • Swelling in the baseboards, or bubbled paint just above them
  • A squishy feel under your feet, or wet socks after walking across the room
  • Days after the water was cleaned up, the carpet is cool and clammy to the touch
  • Yellow or brown discoloration rising to the fiber tips while the carpet dries
  • A sour, musty smell that gets stronger when the room is closed up

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Mold on the Backing

Growth usually starts on the underside of the carpet, where it is dark and damp. Your nose finds it well before anything shows on the surface.

Subfloor Damage

Particleboard and OSB under carpet swell and crumble when they stay wet. Squeaks, soft spots and raised seams show up weeks after the spill is forgotten.

Clean Water Turns Dirty

Water that started clean does not stay that way. Soil and bacteria build up in it inside the carpet and pad, and a carpet that was dryable can end up being torn out.

Soaked Pad Underneath

A carpet pad absorbs many times what it weighs in water. The carpet over it and the floor under it keep getting damp from the pad, even once the surface seems dry.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Water Spreads Sideways

The pad wicks water well past the visible wet spot. It moves under walls and into the next room, and the tack strip starts soaking it up.

24 to 48 hours

Smell and Mold Start

Bacteria in the damp pad start producing that sour wet carpet smell. Dust held down in the fibers and the carpet backing both give mold a place to begin.

Day 3 to 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

The latex that holds the backing together breaks down, so the carpet delaminates and ripples. Nails in the tack strip start rusting, and the subfloor swells.

Past one week

Too Late to Save

By now the carpet and pad usually have to come out. The work gets bigger, since mold is often on the subfloor and the lower edge of the drywall.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Find the Edges

Our first job is finding the outer limit of the water, under walls and in closets too, using a thermal camera and moisture meters. We also identify the water category, because that decides what can stay.

2

Extract the Water

With weighted extractors, carpet and the pad beneath it are pressed and emptied in one pass. This removes far more than a wand alone, and it shortens the drying time that follows.

3

Dry All the Layers

Some jobs call for floating the carpet on a cushion of air, others for lifting it and taking the pad out. Air movers and dehumidifiers run until carpet and subfloor both read dry.

4

Clean and Reinstall

Last, we apply antimicrobial to carpet and subfloor if they need it, replace any pad that was taken out, stretch the carpet back over the tack strip, and give it a cleaning.
