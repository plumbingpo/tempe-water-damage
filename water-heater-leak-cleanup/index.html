Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Tempe: What to Know

Even though the heater itself contains 40 to 80 gallons, the leak is not capped at that amount. Because the supply line feeds the tank, a rusted out bottom drains and refills at the same pace. It will run until the cold water valve gets closed. Where the unit sits decides the damage: a garage floor, a hallway closet or, worst of all, an attic above finished rooms.

Our Tempe team extracts the water, then looks at what surrounded the heater. The platform beneath the tank and the base of the closet walls are usually soaked. In a garage, water reaches the wall shared with the house and anything stored on the floor. Water from an attic unit soaks through insulation, then through the ceiling. Our crew takes readings in every area and sets drying equipment to bring it back to normal.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the Heat SourceElectric units get shut down at the breaker. For gas, turn the control knob to off. A burner or heating element must not stay on once the tank is empty.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveFind the cold water shutoff above the heater and turn it clockwise. A stuck or leaking valve means going to the house main and shutting the water off there.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterWhat comes out of the tank can cause scalds. Let pooled water cool off before anyone steps in it, and keep kids and pets out of the area.
  4. Drain the Tank SafelyA garden hose on the drain valve, if you can get to it safely, lets you send what is left in the tank outside or into a floor drain instead of across the floor.
  5. Move Stored ItemsGet rugs, boxes and other absorbent things up off the wet floor. Because cardboard and fabric take in water quickly, they hold dampness against the walls and the slab.
Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • A missing expansion tank or a failed thermostat that lets too much pressure build inside.
  • Loose plumbing connections left after a recent installation or repair.
  • A drain valve that loosens, cracks or no longer closes all the way.
  • A blocked or rusted drain pan under an attic or closet heater that overflows instead of draining.
  • Corroded inlet and outlet fittings or flex connectors on top of the heater.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Items Stored Near the Heater

Boxes, furniture and keepsakes stored near a garage or closet heater sit in the water first. Mold can develop on damp contents that are not promptly dried or tossed, while cardboard boxes disintegrate.

Adjacent Room Flooring

Carpet, hardwood or laminate in the hallway gets wet as water escapes below the closet door. Heat makes it worse, with quicker swelling of wood boards and carpet backing that comes apart.

Attic and Ceilings

When an attic heater leaks, water passes through the insulation and lands on the ceiling underneath. Soaked insulation loses its insulating value and gets heavy enough to collapse the drywall ceiling.

Stand and Subfloor Damage

More water goes into the subfloor or wooden stand beneath the tank than anywhere else. That wood may rot and lose strength, and it will have to carry the weight of a new tank filled with water.

What We Do

Our Water Heater Leak Cleanup Process

1

Remove Standing Water

Standing water is taken up from the garage, closet or floor. With an attic unit we also clear the ceiling cavity and overflow pan, which keeps nearby materials from soaking up more.

2

Check Around the Heater

We go over the wall bases, the platform, any ceiling underneath and the adjoining rooms with thermal imaging and moisture meters, which shows how far the wet area extends.

3

Take Out What Will Not Dry

Damaged baseboards, drywall that has started to crumble and saturated insulation all get removed. In attics we clear wet insulation so the ceiling joists and drywall below can be dried.

We keep the drying equipment going for the usual 3 to 5 days needed to dry the space. Our crew logs the readings and lets you know as soon as the area is ready for a replacement heater.

Coverage

Does Insurance Cover It?

Water damage to belongings, walls and floors from a tank that fails suddenly is often covered as a sudden and accidental loss. The water heater itself usually is not, since wearing out with age is considered maintenance. Because coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks, a claim may be denied or reduced when the adjuster sees indications the tank leaked for a long time, like old stains or trails of rust. Read your policy, take photos of the damage and the heater, and do not dispose of the old unit before the claim is settled.

Cost

What Affects the Cost

  • How much insulation, ceiling or wall drywall needs to be taken out.
  • How many stored belongings got wet and need to be moved, dried or hauled away.
  • Condition of the stand or subfloor below the heater, which may need rebuilding before the replacement is set.
  • The location of the heater, since a garage leak costs less than one in an interior closet or attic.
