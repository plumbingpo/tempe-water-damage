Bathroom Water Damage in Tempe

Tile, tubs and fixtures are made to get wet on top, but what lies beneath them is not. Every flush and every shower lets a small amount of water by after the caulk cracks, the grout wears down or the seal at the toilet fails. That water gathers where you cannot see it, in the subfloor and inside the tiled wall. We help Tempe homeowners by tracing the leak path, checking its reach with meters, and drying or removing whatever got wet.

Slow leaks are the norm here, which means what you find has typically been there longer, and gone further, than it seems. Subfloor under a toilet can be soft all the way through while the tile above still looks fine. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet area without tearing out more than we need to, then open only the sections that cannot be dried in place.

Warning Signs

  • A musty smell that comes back no matter how often the room is cleaned
  • Directly under the bathroom, a ceiling with a brown stain or a sag
  • On the wall beside the shower, baseboard that has swelled or paint that is peeling
  • Dark stains at the base of the toilet, or a bowl that wobbles a little
  • Grout lines that stay dark and damp long after the last shower
  • Floor tiles that sound hollow, rock underfoot, or have come loose near the tub

What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Pinpoint the Leak

We test the tub, shower, toilet and supply lines one at a time, and use moisture meters and thermal imaging to see where water has traveled under tile and inside walls.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

Soft wall board, loose tile and subfloor that has rotted are removed. If the toilet was the source, we handle that section as contaminated, and porous materials we cannot clean go.

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the subfloor, the wall framing and the ceiling space below. Before anything gets closed up, antimicrobial is applied and dry readings are confirmed.

After replacing backer board and subfloor, we can arrange tile work, a new seal at the toilet, fresh caulking and ceiling repair, which closes the leak path for real.

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Damage Downstairs

The stain on the lower ceiling can be several feet off from the leak itself, because water travels along pipes and joists first. Its path can cross electrical wiring and ceiling lights.

Trapped Steam

Without a working exhaust fan, the steam from showers has nowhere to go. It condenses on the ceiling and walls, encourages mold, and keeps already wet materials from drying.

Leaking Shower Pan

With a crack in the liner or pan, water escapes below the shower floor at each use. Framing under the shower stays saturated even though the surface shows no flaw.

Mold Behind Tile

Neither grout nor tile is a waterproof barrier without help. When the layer behind them stays wet, mold grows on the wall board where cleaning never reaches.

The Damage Timeline

First days

Water Slips Through

With every shower, the bad caulk or grout lets a small amount of water through. Nothing shows yet, but the subfloor edge and the board behind the tile are absorbing it.

First few weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Within the wall cavity, and across the bottom of the floor, mold begins.

One to three months

Signs Start to Appear

You see darker grout, loosening tiles and blackened caulk. If there is a room below, a stain appears on its ceiling and slowly widens.

Six months and beyond

The Structure Suffers

Around the tub or toilet the subfloor goes soft, and the joists may start rotting. At this point the job means new tile and carpentry on top of the drying.
