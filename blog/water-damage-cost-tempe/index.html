How Much Does Water Damage Cost in Tempe?

Home / Blog / How Much Does Water Damage Cost in Tempe?

The Quick Answer

Most water damage jobs in Tempe fall somewhere between $1,500 and $10,000 or more. Small clean-water jobs can come in under about $1,500. At the other end, heavy flooding or sewage with reconstruction work can go well beyond $10,000.

None of this is a quote, only the usual range across the country. Pricing in Tempe, AZ depends on local wages and the going rate for materials, so your number may differ. The only way to get an exact figure is an on-site inspection with moisture readings.

How Water Type Affects Cost

Water type is normally the starting point when a restoration job is priced. The industry sorts water into three categories based on how contaminated it is. As contamination rises, so does the need for cleaning, tear-out of materials and protective equipment.

Depending on which category applies, US cleanup costs are usually in the area of $3 to $7.50 a square foot. The table below shows common rates.

CategoryWhat it isTypical cost per sq ft
Category 1 (clean water)Supply lines, faucets, water heater tanksAbout $3 to $4
Category 2, gray waterFailed sump pumps, discharge from a dishwasher or washing machineRoughly $4 to $6.50
Category 3 (black water)Storm surge, flooding rivers, sewageAbout $7 to $7.50

Category can change. Leave clean water standing a couple of days and bacteria grow, so it gets handled as black or gray water. That is one reason waiting costs money.

What Affects the Cost Most

An identical pipe break in two houses can lead to bills that are far apart. These are the items a contractor weighs in an estimate.

  • Area affected. Labor, equipment and time on site all grow with the amount of wet square footage.
  • Water category. Jobs with clean water cost the least. The highest prices come with sewage.
  • How long it sat. The longer water sits, the deeper it gets into walls and the subfloor. A leak found in an hour costs far less than one found after a weekend away.
  • Materials. Tile and concrete dry well. Insulation, drywall, carpet pad and hardwood frequently have to be replaced or dried with special methods.
  • Mold. If growth has started, mold remediation is added to the scope, with containment and air filtration.
  • Reconstruction. One part of the bill is drying. Afterward comes a second cost for new flooring, drywall, paint and cabinets, which on larger jobs often exceeds the first.

An estimate normally has two main parts, with water extraction coming before structural drying. Plan on 3 to 5 days for the drying in most cases.

Air movers and dehumidifiers are often billed by the day. If your house is dry after three days, you pay less than if it needs six.

Price Ranges by Type of Job

Below are the jobs our team handles most in Tempe, with the national range that is typical for each. They are a place to begin, and an actual price will differ.

  • Burst pipe. Often $1,500 to $5,000 for cleanup and drying, plus the burst pipe repair itself. Expect more if it ran for hours or soaked more than one floor.
  • Appliance leak. Found early, a leak from a dishwasher, water heater or washing machine is often $1,000 to $4,000. When a slow drip goes unnoticed below cabinets, concealed damage raises the price.
  • Basement flood. The usual range is $2,000 to $10,000 or more, varying with the water's source, its depth and any finished surfaces. Basement water removal in a finished basement sits at the high end.
  • Sewage backup. Many jobs cost $3,000 to $10,000, some more. Anything porous has to go and all surfaces get disinfected, since cleaning up sewage counts as Category 3.
  • Roof leak. Interior work on wet ceilings, walls and insulation often comes to $1,000 to $5,000, and the roof fix is extra.

Will Insurance Pay for Water Damage in Tempe?

Two things decide it: the cause of the loss and your policy's wording. Sudden, accidental damage, like a supply line failing without warning or a burst pipe, is often covered by a standard homeowners policy.

Coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks. An insurer may treat a fitting that dripped for months as a maintenance problem. Outside flooding, for instance from storm surge or a river that rises, is usually not covered without separate flood insurance. Sewer backup often needs its own add-on.

Before assuming anything, read the policy or phone your agent. Your deductible also matters. A small repair may come in under the deductible, in which case filing may not be worthwhile.

How to Keep the Bill Down

How much you pay for water damage is largely settled within a day or two. You can change the outcome with a handful of easy steps.

  • Act fast. Water keeps spreading until it is removed. Each additional hour soaks more of the building.
  • Shut off the water. Know where your main valve is and close it as soon as you find a leak.
  • Document everything. Shoot video and pictures first, while everything is still in place and nothing has been tossed. Save every receipt.
  • Call a pro before mold starts. Mold can begin growing in 24 to 48 hours. After that point, expect added time and added cost.
  • Move what you can. If you can do it safely, lift boxes, furniture and rugs clear of the wet floor.

Do not walk through standing water if the power is on, and stay out of anything that may contain sewage. The risk of being shocked or getting sick outweighs anything you might save. If the water came from outside, leave flood cleanup to a crew with the right protective gear.

Turning a Range Into an Estimate

Averages from across the country are fine for planning and still cannot describe your home. Only a moisture meter inspection reveals where the water went and what drying and repairs will be needed.
